# "We already have an equivalent" is a quality verdict with no quality measured

**The rule.** Having something in the catalogue proves it **exists**. It does not
prove it **delivers the same thing**. Dismissing a new option as redundant is a
judgement about quality, and it requires actually comparing outputs.

## The shape of the failure

Sixteen tools are evaluated. Twelve get marked redundant, with reasons like *"tool
A does the same as tool B, which we already have"*.

Neither side was run. What was compared was the **category** — both do upscaling,
both do parallax — and category was treated as equivalence.

Two tools in the same category can differ from each other more than one of them
differs from doing nothing at all.

## Why dismissal is more dangerous than adoption

Adopting a bad tool is loud: it fails, someone notices, it gets removed.

**Dismissing a good one is silent.** The item leaves the table looking like a
reasoned decision, nobody revisits it, and the cost never shows up as a cost — it
shows up as work that stayed harder than it needed to be, indefinitely.

## The sibling mistake, worth naming separately

Reporting as **"tested and rejected"** something that was actually **rejected by
reading its documentation**. Those are different claims and the second one gets
laundered into the first almost automatically, because the conclusion is the
same and only the provenance differs.

Three states, three different words, and they must not be mixed:

- **dismissed by reading** — I read what it does and decided it does not fit
- **to compare** — plausible, not yet run
- **tested** — I ran both sides on the same input and looked at the outputs

## The cheap discipline

Before writing "redundant", name the input you would give both, and what you
would look at in the two outputs. If you cannot describe that in one sentence,
the honest word is *to compare*.
