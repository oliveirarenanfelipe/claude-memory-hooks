# The mechanism working is not the quality shipped

**The rule.** Proving that your technique functions — and passing the checks you
wrote for it — establishes that the mechanism works. It says nothing about whether
the output is any good **compared to what already exists**. Those are different
claims, and only the second one is what you are delivering.

## The shape of the failure

A generator is built. The technique is sound, the pipeline runs end to end, and
the quality gate passes on both test subjects. It is presented as done.

The response: *"the thing you can do, the rest of the world is doing much
better."*

The distance had a number available the whole time — **40 frames against 148** in
a comparable reference. Nobody looked until after the delivery was rejected. The
gate had been measuring whether the machine ran, which it did, perfectly.

*(Illustrative case.)*

## Why an internal gate cannot catch this

A gate you wrote encodes what you already know to check. Its ceiling is your own
current standard — so it can confirm you met your standard, and it is structurally
incapable of telling you your standard is below the field's.

The reference is external by definition. No amount of internal rigour substitutes
for it.

## The practice

**Opening a real reference is a requirement of the brief, not consolation after
rejection.** Before building, find two or three examples of the thing done well
by someone else, and write down what makes them good in terms you can measure.
Those numbers become the gate.

The version of this that actually works is uncomfortable: put your output and the
reference side by side, in the same view, at the same size, before showing anyone
anything.

## The tell

If your evidence for quality consists entirely of checks you designed, you have
evidence about the mechanism. Quality evidence has someone else's work in it.
