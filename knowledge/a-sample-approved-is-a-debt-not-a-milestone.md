# A sample approved is a debt, not a milestone

**The rule.** Settling the approach on a short excerpt is a good way to work and a
dangerous place to stop. The moment the sample is approved, a debt opens: **the
rest of the artefact is still raw**, and nothing in the artefact will tell you.

## The shape of the failure

The agreement was sensible: get the first thirty seconds right, lock the
approach, then apply it to the rest. The first thirty seconds were built,
measured and approved. The conversation moved on to the next subject.

Three separate treatments stayed frozen at the sample:

| treatment | covered | left raw |
|---|---|---|
| audio processing | 34 s | **289 s (89%)** |
| music bed | 34 s | **289 s (89%)** |
| shot variation | 24 s | **299 s (93%)** |

None of the three surfaced through a check, a test or a re-read. All three
surfaced because someone asked *"how are we doing on that?"*. Without the
question, it ships with 89% of the audio untreated.

*(Illustrative case.)*

## Why it is invisible

The sample is **perfect** — it was worked, measured and approved. Whatever you
look at first is the good part, and it is the part everyone has in mind when they
think about the piece. The raw remainder is not broken in any way that announces
itself; it is simply untouched.

And the approval creates a genuine feeling of completion. Something *was* finished:
the decision. The mind files the whole task under that.

## The practice

**When a sample is approved, write the extension down as an open item in the same
breath** — before the conversation moves on. Not "apply the treatment": the
specific number, "289 seconds of audio still raw".

The number is what makes it survive. "Apply to the rest" reads as done the moment
the approach is settled; "289 seconds still raw" does not.

## The general form

Any approval scoped to a subset creates an obligation for the complement. Pilot
approved, rollout pending. One endpoint migrated, eleven to go. The approval is
never the whole thing, and the excitement of agreement is exactly when everyone
stops counting.
