# An external document never asserts the unverified

**The rule.** A piece that leaves your organisation — a report, a proposal, a
filing, a message to a client — must not state as fact anything that only a
**future** verification would establish.

Inside your own notes, a working assumption is useful and normal. The moment it
crosses the boundary it stops being an assumption and becomes a commitment
someone else will act on.

## The shape of the failure

A report describes a corrective action and states that a monitoring routine "runs
continuously and would have detected the condition". The routine exists. Whether
it was running during the period in question was never checked — it was assumed,
reasonably, because it was scheduled.

The recipient now has a factual claim from you about a period you did not verify.
If they check and it was down, the cost is not the error. The cost is that
everything else in the document becomes suspect.

## The two-pass read before anything goes out

**First pass — mark every factual claim.** Anything with a number, a date, a
state, or a capability.

**Second pass — for each mark, name what would prove it.** If the answer is "I
would have to go and check", either check it, or rewrite the sentence to say what
you actually know.

## The rewrite is usually easy and costs nothing

- *"the routine detects this condition"* → *"the routine is configured to detect
  this condition; we are confirming its coverage for the period"*
- *"the data is complete"* → *"the data covers the period from X to Y"*

The second version is stronger, not weaker. Precision reads as competence;
overreach reads as competence right up until someone checks.
